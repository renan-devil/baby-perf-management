# The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse

Once upon a time, at the edge of a wheat field, under the roots of an old apple tree, there lived a little Country Mouse.

Her home was small and cosy. She had a bed made of soft green moss, a cup made from an acorn, and a little round window that looked out over the fields. In the morning she listened to the birds singing. In the evening she watched the sun go down behind the hills. Country Mouse was very happy.

One day, a letter arrived. It was written in very curly writing, and it smelled of perfume.

*“Dear Cousin,”* it said. *“I shall come and visit you on Sunday. Yours, Town Mouse.”*

“My cousin from the big city!” cried Country Mouse. “I must get everything ready!”

She swept the floor. She shook out her moss bed. And she went out to find the very best things to eat: some grains of wheat, a few barley seeds, a juicy blackberry, a hazelnut, and a little piece of cheese that she had been saving for a special day.

On Sunday, Town Mouse arrived. He wore a smart waistcoat with shiny buttons, a silk bow tie and a tall top hat. He looked around the little house and wrinkled his nose.

“Goodness me,” he said. “How very… small.”

Country Mouse was very proud of her lunch. She set everything out on a big leaf. “Please, Cousin, help yourself!”

Town Mouse nibbled one grain of wheat. He nibbled a barley seed. He poked the blackberry with the tip of his paw. “Hmm,” he said. “Is this all you eat? Seeds and berries? Every single day?”

“Well… yes,” said Country Mouse. “They’re very good.”

“My poor cousin,” said Town Mouse, shaking his head. “You don’t know what you’re missing! In the city, I eat cakes and cheeses and sweets every day. My house is a palace! You must come and stay with me. Then you’ll see how a mouse should really live.”

Country Mouse looked at her little house, and at her simple lunch. Suddenly they both seemed a bit too small. “All right,” she said. “I’ll come!”

So the very next morning, the two mice set off. They hid in a basket of cabbages on the back of the farmer’s cart, and they bumped and rattled along the road for a long, long time. *Clip-clop, clip-clop*, went the horse. *Bump, bump, bump*, went the cart.

At last they arrived in the city. Country Mouse had never seen anything like it! There were houses as tall as mountains. There were carriages and bicycles, and people everywhere, hurrying and shouting. *Ding-ding!* went a tram. *Honk! Honk!* went a car. Country Mouse held on tightly to her cousin’s tail so that she wouldn’t get lost.

“Here we are,” said Town Mouse proudly. They squeezed under a big front door and into a very grand house. There were thick carpets, golden mirrors, and a chandelier that sparkled like a thousand stars. And in the dining room there was a long table covered with the most wonderful food Country Mouse had ever seen.

There were cakes with pink icing. There was a cheese as big as a cartwheel, full of holes. There were grapes and pears and peaches, chocolate biscuits, jellies that wobbled, and a great big bowl of cream.

“Help yourself, Cousin!” said Town Mouse. “Isn’t this better than seeds and berries?”

Country Mouse’s eyes were as round as buttons. She climbed up the tablecloth, sat down beside the cheese and took a big bite. “Mmmm!” she said. It was delicious!

But just then, the door swung open. *BANG!* In came the cook, holding a big wooden spoon. “Who’s there?” she shouted. “Mice! MICE in my dining room!”

“Quick! Hide!” squeaked Town Mouse. The two mice jumped down from the table and dashed into a little hole in the wall. Country Mouse’s heart was going *boom-boom-boom*.

They waited and waited, as quiet as can be, until the cook went away.

“It’s all right now,” whispered Town Mouse. “That happens all the time. Come on, let’s try the cake!”

They crept back to the table on tiptoe. Country Mouse had just put her paw into the pink icing when… “Miaaaaow!”

A big ginger cat jumped up onto a chair. He had yellow eyes, long whiskers and very sharp claws. And he was looking straight at them.

“RUN!” cried Town Mouse. The two mice raced across the table, knocking over the jelly — *splosh!* — slid along the tablecloth, jumped down to the floor and dived into the hole in the wall, just in time. The cat’s paw came in after them, feeling about — *scratch, scratch* — but the hole was much too small.

Country Mouse was shaking from the tips of her ears to the end of her tail. They waited for a long, long time, until the cat got bored and went off to sleep by the fire.

“Phew,” said Town Mouse, straightening his bow tie. “Now, shall we try the peaches?”

But before they could take a single step, there came a loud *WOOF! WOOF! WOOF!* Two big dogs came bounding into the dining room, sniffing and barking, their tails knocking over the chairs.

Country Mouse covered her eyes. “Oh dear, oh dear, oh dear,” she whispered.

When at last the house was quiet again, Country Mouse picked up her little bag.

“Thank you very much for having me, Cousin,” she said politely. “Your house is very grand, and your food is very fine. But I’m going home.”

“Going home?” said Town Mouse. “But why? You haven’t even tried the cream!”

“I would rather eat my seeds and berries in peace,” said Country Mouse, “than eat cake and cheese and be frightened all the time.”

So she said goodbye, climbed back into the cabbage basket on the farmer’s cart, and bumped and rattled all the way home. *Clip-clop, clip-clop. Bump, bump, bump.*

When she saw the old apple tree at the edge of the wheat field, her heart gave a happy little jump. She ran inside her cosy little house. She made herself a supper of wheat grains and blackberries, and it tasted better than all the cakes in the world.

Then she curled up in her soft moss bed. Outside, the stars came out one by one, and an owl called *“Hoo-hoo.”* There were no cooks, no cats and no dogs. Only the wind in the wheat, going *shhhh, shhhh*.

“Home,” said Country Mouse with a great big yawn. “There’s nowhere better.” And she fell fast asleep.

> A simple life in peace is worth more than a rich life full of fear. There’s no place like home.
