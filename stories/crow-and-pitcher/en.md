# The Crow and the Pitcher

It was the hottest day of the whole summer. The sun blazed, the grass turned yellow, and every pond and puddle had dried up.

A black crow flew over the fields. “Caw!” she croaked. “I’m so thirsty! If I don’t find some water soon, I won’t be able to fly another inch.”

At last she spotted a tall clay pitcher standing in a garden. She landed on its edge and peeked inside. There was water! But only a little, right at the very bottom.

The crow pushed her beak in as far as it would go. She stretched her neck. She stretched it some more. *Tap!* Her beak touched only the side of the pitcher. The water was too far down.

She tried to tip the pitcher over. *Heave!* It was far too heavy.

So the crow sat down and thought very hard. Then she looked at the little pebbles lying all over the garden path.

Can you guess what she did? *(Pause, and let Constance guess.)*

She picked up a pebble in her beak and dropped it into the pitcher. *Plop!* Then another one. *Plop!* And another. *Plop! Plop! Plop!*

With every pebble, the water rose a tiny bit higher. Up, and up, and up it came, until it reached the very top.

The crow dipped in her beak and drank, and drank, until she wasn’t thirsty any more. “Caw!” she said happily, and off she flew into the blue sky.

> When something doesn’t work one way, stop, think, and try another way. Little by little, you get there!
